\begin{problem}{Focus Streak}{standard input}{standard output}{1 second}{256 megabytes}

Manish wants to stay productive, so every day he needs to complete work worth $k$ focus points. On day $i$ he earns $a_i$ focus points.

Manish chooses a non-empty segment of consecutive days $[l, r]$ and starts it with a balance equal to $0$. On each day $i$ of the segment, his balance changes by $a_i - k$: if $a_i > k$, the surplus is saved, and if $a_i < k$, the shortage is borrowed.

A segment is called \textit{sustainable} if after \textbf{each} day of it the balance is not less than $-m$. Formally, a segment $[l, r]$ is sustainable if for every $j$ from $l$ to $r$,
$$\sum_{i=l}^{j} (a_i - k) \ge -m.$$

Find the length of the longest sustainable segment, or report that there is none.

\InputFile
The first line contains three integers $n$, $k$ and $m$ ($1 \le n \le 10^5$; $0 \le k \le 10^4$; $0 \le m \le 10^9$) --- the number of days, the daily requirement, and the maximum allowed debt.

The second line contains $n$ integers $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n$ ($0 \le a_i \le 10^4$) --- the focus points earned on each day.

\OutputFile
Print a single integer --- the length of the longest sustainable segment. If there are no sustainable segments, print $0$.

\Note
In the first example, the segment $[4, 6]$ (the days with $9, 2, 2$) is sustainable: the balances after each day are $5, 3, 1$, and all of them are at least $-2$. The segment $[1, 3]$ is not sustainable: the balances are $2, -1, -4$, and $-4 < -2$. No segment of length $4$ or more is sustainable.

In the second example, the segment $[3, 6]$ (the days with $7, 0, 3, 5$) is sustainable: the balances are $3, -1, -2, -1$, all at least $-3$. Adding day $7$ gives balance $-4 < -3$. No segment of length $5$ or more is sustainable.

In the third example, already on day $1$ the balance becomes $1 - 5 = -4 < 0$, and every other day is similar, so there are no sustainable segments.

\end{problem}
